Access

Who can see what

Two kinds of people use this, and only one of them has an account.

Organizers

Sign in with Google, and are then let in only if somebody has put them on the roster. Everyone who works a screen holds one of these, base ops included.

Volunteers

Open their own link. No account, no password, no session — and nothing to set up for a fourteen-year-old on a borrowed phone.

Getting somebody in

An organizer invites somebody by email address from the Access screen. When that person signs in with that address, they are in. No console, no waiting on whoever set the project up.

Invitations tidy themselves up: one that has already been used, or that belongs to somebody who is now on the roster by another route, is cleared the next time they sign in. Otherwise the "waiting to sign in" list fills up with people who are already here, and a list like that stops being read.

Admins and organizers

Almost everything is open to organizers, including things that sound risky and are not. Finding a shop's address is wrong happens standing outside the shop, and the person standing there should be able to fix it. Wanting the support number changed at eight on the Saturday morning is not unreasonable.

What stays with an admin is the work that cannot be undone or noticed later:

  • Who gets in. Adding and removing people from the roster.
  • Starting or deleting a year. Deleting one takes its jars, shifts and records with it.
  • Removing a shop from the library. Years of records hang off it, and an orphaned year cannot be spotted afterwards.
  • Renaming the sections. Every past year's figures are grouped by these, so a rename quietly changes how all of them read.
  • Reading the audit log. A record about the organizers themselves.

A volunteer's link

A pass link is a long random string, and holding it is the whole of the credential. It reaches exactly one page — that person's own shifts, where to report, and who to ring. There is no way to list them, so knowing one link tells you nothing about any other, and no way to walk from a pass to anybody else's details.

Links are made fresh for each event, so last year's does not open this year's.

A pass names the shop only after an organizer has checked that person in. Everybody reports to base first, so nothing is lost by waiting — and an email cannot be taken back once it has gone.

Each line says what changed and what it said before — not just that something happened. Changes to shared things like the shop library are kept too, filed against no particular year.

Every change is recorded: who made it, when, what it was, and what it said before. Money that was corrected, a child marked as a no-show and then unmarked, somebody granted access, a shop renamed.

Entries cannot be edited or deleted by anybody, including an admin. That is the point — "who removed last year" is exactly the question a log exists to answer, so deleting a year cannot take its record with it, and the app says so before you do it.

The log records names, section and pairing. It never records a parent's phone number or email address: it is read by admins and kept for years, and no question anybody asks of it needs those.

What is deliberately not here

  • No public schedule. There is no page listing children and the shops they will be standing outside.
  • No shared device role. Anybody recording money signs in as themselves, so there is nobody holding a role that does not appear in a list.
  • No parent accounts. Nothing to create, remember or reset.

Nothing in the database is readable without either an account on the roster or one specific volunteer's link.
