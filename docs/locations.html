The places

Locations

Shops are recorded once and reused every year, so their history follows them.

The library

One entry per real place, shared by every year: the address, a map link, who to ask for at the shop, its insurance paperwork, and any note worth passing on — which door to stand at, where not to block, who to check in with on arrival.

Opening hours are recorded for every day of the week, whether or not you staff that day. That is what lets the schedule warn you about a locked door.

Also known as

A shop gets written down differently by different people, and across years the spelling drifts. Recording the other names it has gone by means a shop's history stays on one row rather than splitting into two half-shops, each with half the takings and neither ranking properly.

Which shops a year uses

Each year picks from the library and puts them in order, with a switch for each. A shop that closed is switched off rather than deleted, so the years it did run still read correctly.

The order is a priority: work this one first. It changes year to year, so it belongs to the year rather than to the shop.

All of them on one map

A year's shops, laid out as they actually sit. It answers the questions a list cannot: which two are close enough for one person to cover both, whether base is anywhere sensible in the middle of them, and whether a whole side of town has been forgotten.

Organizers only. This is exactly the thing a volunteer’s link is written to avoid saying — see who can see what.

The map and the list beside it are two views of one thing. Point at a name and its pin lights up; point at a pin and its name does. A numbered pin only answers “which of these is Corner Grocers” if you go hunting for the number, and a list of nineteen names says nothing about where any of them is — each is half an answer until they are joined up.

It follows the keyboard as well as the pointer, so tabbing down the list walks the map with you.

Where the pins come from

From each shop’s address, looked up once and remembered. A button on the locations screen works through the ones that have not been placed yet, a second at a time, and says how long that will take before you press it — the service doing the looking up is free and run on donated hardware, so it is asked politely.

Anything it cannot find is named rather than quietly left off, because a shop it could not place usually means an address worth checking. A shop with no address at all is counted separately: that is a different problem, and it is one somebody has to type.

A shop's own page

Every shop name in the app is a link. Following one gives you everything about that place in one screen:

  • Arranging it — the address, a map, when it is open, who to ask for, and the notes.
  • Year by year — what it raised each year, and the hours it took, so a shop quietly falling off is visible.
  • Hour by hour, year on year — which hours are worth staffing here, which is not the same answer as for the event overall.
  • This event — who is scheduled, and when.
  • Jars counted here — every jar, with what was in it.
