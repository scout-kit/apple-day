Why the place is missing

Everybody reports to base before going anywhere, so a volunteer does not need to know the shop in advance. And a link like this gets forwarded — to a grandparent, into a group chat, to whoever is doing the lift. A page that names a child and says exactly where they will be standing for the next two hours is not something to hand around.

Once they have arrived and been checked in, the page names the shop and shows the route from base.

Getting the link to somebody

Every volunteer's link comes with a code that opens it. It shows up wherever that person is looked up, not only on a printed sheet — because the usual way this comes up is a parent ringing to say the link does not work, and whoever answers wants to read it back, send it again, or hold the code up to their phone.

There is also a printable sheet of all of them, for a group that would rather hand out paper at a meeting than email anything.

The code is drawn in the browser. The link is the credential, so it never goes to anybody's server to be turned into a picture.

Asking for a swap

A volunteer who cannot make a shift can say so from their own page, without an account. It arrives as a request an organizer can see, deal with, and keep. Requests are kept rather than deleted, because "I told you on Wednesday" is a conversation that needs an answer.

Reminders

Preview · Saturday 10:00, those not yet checked in
To: parent@example.org Subject: Your Saturday 10:00 AM shift at Apple Day
Hi Jordan, A reminder about Saturday 10:00 AM for Amara and Eli: Amara: Saturday 10:00 AM – 11:00 AM Amara's page: https://example.org/p/k3Ns8pQ2 Eli: Saturday 10:00 AM – 11:00 AM Eli's page: https://example.org/p/9wRt4mXb On the day, ring base ops on 555 0100.

One message per parent, however many children they have here — with a link for each. Times, never places.

Reminders are sent from an organizer's own mailbox, so they arrive from somebody the parents recognise and replies reach a human rather than a no-reply address. There is no server to send from, and this turns out to be the better arrangement anyway.

You choose who a reminder goes to — everybody, a day, or one hour — and which of three messages to send: here is your schedule, your shift is coming up, or we are expecting you and have not seen you yet. That last one is only ever sent to people who have not checked in, and cannot be sent to anybody else.

Somebody working two hours back to back does not get a second reminder an hour in. Checking in covers the whole stretch.

The wording is yours

Each of the three messages can be rewritten in the group's own voice, with a live preview showing a real example as you type. What cannot be changed is who a message is allowed to reach, and no amount of editing can put a shop's address into an email.

What has already gone out

The app keeps a record of who has been sent what, so a second pass through the same list does not mail the same parent twice. If you do want to send again, you have to say so.

If email is not set up, or a sign-in is refused on the morning itself, the same messages come out as a file to hand to whatever mailing tool the group already uses. Nobody misses out because a consent screen misbehaved.
